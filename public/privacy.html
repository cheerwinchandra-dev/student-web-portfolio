Who sees your data

Only I read your messages. To run the site I use these service providers (“Data Processors”). They handle data only to provide their service to me, under their terms of service and privacy policies (and data processing agreements, where my plan includes one), which require them to protect it:

  • Vercel Inc. (United States): hosts and runs this website.
  • Neon (a Postgres database service): stores the messages and records described above, encrypted, in the data-centre region chosen when the database was set up.
  • Google (Gmail): when I reply to you by email, the reply and the thread pass through my email provider.
  • GitHub, Inc.: the site shows my public GitHub projects. My server fetches them from GitHub; no visitor data is sent. Only if my server is down and you have allowed it in Privacy choices, your browser loads them from GitHub directly, and GitHub then sees your IP address.

Some of these providers store data outside India. The DPDP Act allows this except to countries the Government of India restricts, and I will follow any such restriction. I will also disclose data if the law requires it, for example to a court or a government authority with a valid legal request.

Your rights, and how to use them

Under sections 11 to 14 of the DPDP Act you can ask me to:

  • Show you a summary of the personal data I hold about you, what I do with it, and who I have shared it with.
  • Correct, complete or update it.
  • Delete it, and withdraw your consent. I will then stop using it and delete your messages and consent record, keeping only the short access-log note described above.
  • Nominate someone to use these rights for you if you die or can no longer do so yourself.
  • Raise a grievance about how I handle your data.

How: email me at cheerwinchandra@gmail.com, or send the contact form with the topic “Privacy / data request”. Include your reference number if you have one. Withdrawing consent is as easy as giving it: one email or one message is enough. To protect you, I may ask you to write from the same email address you used before.

When: I will acknowledge your request within 2 working days and complete it within 30 days, and always within the time the DPDP Rules allow.

Not satisfied? Once you have used this grievance process, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India under the DPDP Act.

Your duties. The DPDP Act also asks you not to impersonate anyone, not to hide important information and not to make false or frivolous complaints.

Children

This site is not meant for anyone under 18, and the contact form asks you to confirm you are 18 or older. If you are under 18, please ask a parent or guardian to get in touch for you; I need their consent before I can handle a child’s data (section 9 of the DPDP Act). If I learn that I received a child’s data without that consent, I will delete it. The site never tracks or profiles anyone, including children, and shows no targeted ads.

How I keep your data safe

I take reasonable security safeguards, as the DPDP Act (section 8(5)) and the IT (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011 ask:

  • The whole site uses HTTPS (encrypted connections), enforced with HSTS, plus strict security headers that block injected scripts and framing.
  • Messages are stored in a database encrypted at rest, reached only over encrypted connections.
  • Only I can open the inbox, with a strong password (stored only as a salted PBKDF2-SHA256 hash) and two-step sign-in with codes from an authenticator app. Repeated wrong attempts lock sign-in.
  • IP addresses are never stored in plain form: only a salted one-way fingerprint, or a masked address (like 203.0.x.x) in my access log.
  • An access log records sign-ins, exports and deletions, and is kept for 400 days (more than the one year the rules ask for).
  • Data is deleted automatically when its time is up, and I keep private, regular backups.

If a breach happens, I will tell everyone affected without delay: what happened, what it could mean for you, what I am doing about it, and what you can do. I will inform the Data Protection Board of India, with a detailed report within 72 hours, and report the incident to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing it, as the law requires.
